Team Winton, NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.

How to play: the full explanation of the game is in GUIDE.md and on the How MissionForge works page. The web page was regenerated from GUIDE.md on 2026-11-15 (T062), so both carry the same text, including the "Built versus planned" table.

MissionForge is a cooperative party game for 1 to 10 players who act as a space agency: they vote on every part of a rocket and spacecraft under a money and schedule budget, then fly the mission live. One big screen shows the mission; every phone is a mission-control console and a minigame controller. Mass, power, budget, instruments, orbit and communications each decide whether the mission works, and real spaceflight facts sit inside the mechanics.

Try it

  • Hosted: https://missionforge-spaceapps.onrender.com/play/: press Host a mission on a laptop (desktop Chrome or Chromium), then New mission, and join from a phone by scanning the QR code. No install, account or shared Wi-Fi needed. The site runs on a free instance that sleeps after 15 minutes without visitors; the first page can take about a minute to load. The hosted big screen is the Unity host rebuilt from main on 2026-11-15 (task T055): lobby, mission briefing and contract vote with the real contract data, the Build Bay, countdown and flight view with live telemetry. Three limitations of the hosted big screen in a live room, with the workaround: (1) the Flight Readiness Review shows an empty panel, so the host operator presses PageDown to skip it (the GO/NO-GO poll still runs on the phones); (2) when the flight simulation ends the big screen stays on the Flight screen, so the operator presses PageDown to advance to the debrief; (3) the big-screen debrief then shows sample data (8,172 pts, grade A, sample crew names), not the session's result. The new pre-flight text (briefing, contract vote, FRR) is in English only on the big screen. In T055's run of the hosted service on a local machine with three phones, a room went through the briefing, contract vote, all 22 part votes, countdown and flight with real data (the FRR skipped with PageDown); the public URL itself has not yet been played through with the rebuilt host (see Known limitations). To see the whole phone game without a server, open the scripted session below.
  • Locally, one command (Node 20+, Windows or macOS): npm --prefix tools/demo install, then npm --prefix tools/demo run demo. It prints the big-screen URL, the phone URL and a QR code. Phones on the same Wi-Fi join with the code shown on the big screen; no app, account or chat.
  • No network: /play/client/?mock=1 runs a scripted phone session without a server.
  • Demo videos: submission/video/missionforge-demo-2min.mp4 (2:00) and missionforge-demo-30s.mp4, with English and Arabic subtitles. The phone footage is the real client in its scripted demo session; the big-screen images are stills (submission/video/README.md lists what is scripted).

How a session flows (25 to 40 minutes)

Lobby and room code, briefing for contract C1 Earth Watch (a 150 kg observation satellite in a 550 km Sun-synchronous orbit, 90 M game-dollar budget), 22 part votes with stats and drawbacks, a GO/NO-GO review, the flight with minigames at critical moments, then a debrief with score, facts and the assumptions behind the numbers. The audience joins by QR and sees its own "Audience says" bar; the host lobby has an "Audience counts as one player" switch (off by default, so the audience is advisory unless the host turns it on). The big screen shows the briefing, contract vote, Build Bay with the readiness dashboard, Flight Readiness Review, countdown, flight view (ascent and orbit map) and debrief (T055's live run covered all of these except the orbit map; in a live room the FRR panel is empty and the big-screen debrief shows sample data, see Known limitations), while the votes, GO/NO-GO poll and minigames are played on the phones; the scripted ?mock=1 phone session shows the phone side without a server. The big-screen test stand and design review, and the campaign hub, codex and settings screens, are also in the host, but the Build Bay does not open the test stand or design review by itself.

Physics versus game simplification

Equations and constants are in GAME_DESIGN.md 8.1 and 8.2. Every simplification (2D orbital plane, one exponential atmosphere, no Earth oblateness, probability rolls for failures, game-scaled costs and schedules) is listed in 8.3 and shown in the in-game Codex. Every number is tagged physical, real-world approximate or game value in the assumptions register (GAME_DESIGN.md 19) and in the value_types of each data entry.

Known limitations

  • Hosting: the pages of the hosted site answered with HTTP 200 on 2026-11-15. The hosted smoke test (T048, run on a local copy of the hosted service) passed the judge path (entry page, Unity host, New mission, three phones join) and played a full C1 session to the debrief with a scripted stand-in host; the real Unity host was not played to the debrief, and real Render was not part of that test (orchestration/evidence/T048/RESULTS.md). What T058 saw on the public URL (with the earlier host build) is in orchestration/evidence/T058/live/LIVE_CHECK.md. The rebuilt host (T055) was played from the lobby through the flight with three phones on a local copy of the hosted service, with the FRR skipped by PageDown (orchestration/evidence/T055/); it has not been played through on the public URL. If the hosted site is slow or down, use the one-command local run above. The free instance drops every room when it restarts or redeploys.
  • Big screen: the Unity host on main has 15 of its 16 screens: Title, Lobby, Mission Briefing, Contract Vote, Build Bay, Test Stand, Design Review, Flight Readiness Review, Countdown, Flight, Orbit Map, Debrief, Campaign Hub, Codex and Settings. The minigame overlay has no screen of its own (the flight view shows a minigame banner and slows time; the minigames run on the phones). The committed WebGL build, and so the hosted site, was rebuilt from main by T055, so it should contain all fifteen; its live run covered the screens below. In T055's live run (local copy of the hosted service, three phones) the briefing, contract vote, 22 Build Bay votes, countdown and flight showed real data and the characters ≈, ₂, ×, ±, ° rendered (they were missing boxes before). Known limitations of the hosted big screen in a live room (T055 evidence; not fixed in the merged build): (1) the Flight Readiness Review shows an empty panel, because the Build Bay does not hand its build to it (the "Build Bay hook" was not added in T055): the host operator presses PageDown to skip it, and the GO/NO-GO poll runs on the phones; (2) when the flight simulation ends, the big screen stays on the Flight screen: the operator presses PageDown to advance to the debrief; (3) the big-screen debrief shows sample (fixture) data, 8,172 pts, grade A and sample crew names, not the session's result; (4) the new pre-flight text (host_preflight strings) exists only in English in the hosted build, so the big screen falls back to English in the other languages. The test stand, design review, campaign hub, codex and settings screens were checked in development builds with rehearsal data (T027, T024), not in that run. The Build Bay does not yet open the Test Stand or Design Review by itself, and it builds C1 Earth Watch whatever contract wins the vote. Test status (T058, before the pre-flight translations of T057 were merged): the phone client's translation-parity tests failed (5 of 927) and node tools/validate-data.mjs --strict failed, because data/strings/en.host_preflight.json was English only; T057 (#239) added the five translations, and these two checks have not been re-run since.
  • The flight view ran at a median 59.9 fps in Chrome at 1920x1080 on an Apple M4 Mac in its own test build; the earlier committed host was otherwise loaded in headless Chromium with software rendering. The frame rate of the rebuilt host, and on a typical Windows laptop, has not been measured.
  • A session is planned at 25 to 40 minutes; this is the design estimate, not a measured playtest with six people. The 22 C1 part votes alone take about 17 minutes at the lobby's default timers (46 s a round) or about 8 minutes at the shortest timers (inspect 5 s, vote 10 s: 21 s a round; the rebuilt hosted lobby offers them, the earlier hosted build's shortest vote timer was 20 s), before animation and runoffs.
  • Costs, schedules, reliabilities and boost sizes are game values, not real figures; balance has been checked by a script, not by a large human playtest.
  • The room server keeps rooms in memory only: restarting it loses every room (by design: no accounts, no stored data).
  • Audio is limited: no recordings are bundled.

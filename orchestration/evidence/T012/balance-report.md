## Rules used

- **Valid build.** Every vote of Core `VoteOptions.VotesFor(contract)` is filled with an option from `VoteOptions.Offered`; Core `Validator.Validate(build, catalog, ValidationMode.Complete)` reports no Blocker; `Dashboard.Compute(...).Budget.RemainingM ≥ 0 $M`; and `Budget.TotalDays ≤ Budget.DeadlineDays`. Majors are allowed and counted. Success is always the displayed estimate `DashboardMetrics.Success`, never the hidden truth. No tests, reviews or Emergency Funding are applied (a fresh build).
- **Search.** The propulsion-coupled votes (launch site, both engines, both counts, both tanks, material, strap-on, separation, fairing, booster recovery, heat shield, escape system, capsule and lander, where the contract votes them) are enumerated depth-first with `BuildState.WithChoice`. The architecture votes (first-stage engine, upper-stage engine, strap-on, booster recovery) are decided first; each surviving combination is a *stratum*. The remaining coupled votes follow, with both tanks last. `FixSearch` is not used.
- **Pruning (sound).** A branch is cut on a `ValidationMode.Partial` Blocker. Core judges lift-off TWR and Δv as soon as the engines and tanks are decided, so the tanks are decided last: every other coupled mass, thrust and Δv input is then final, and the votes filled later can only add mass. A branch is also cut when the cost lower bound (`decided parts cost × (1 + largest assurance discount)`) exceeds the budget, or the schedule lower bound (`(longest lead + decided integration days + most negative integration still possible) × (1 + largest assurance schedule discount)`) exceeds the deadline. Part costs are never negative and engine counts are at least 1, so both bounds only grow as votes are decided.
- **Other votes.** Each coupled combination is completed twice, greedily in vote order: the option with the lowest displayed total cost (*cheapest*) and the option with the highest displayed success (*most reliable*). When both give the same build it is evaluated once. The greedy choice is memoized on the offered option sets; this is exact in Core because non-propulsion part costs are additive and context free and displayed success is a product of per-part factors.
- **Cap and sampling.** The evaluation budget is shared equally among the strata still to search (unused budget moves to later strata). A stratum whose subtree fits half its share is enumerated exhaustively; a larger one is sampled with seeded random descents (SplitMix64 from `--seed`, the contract and the stratum) until its share is spent. A contract with any sampled stratum is reported as capped; its counts are for the builds evaluated, not the whole space.
- **Meaningfully different (Decision).** Builds are grouped by architecture `(first-stage engine, upper-stage engine, booster-recovery family, strap-on)`. The recovery families are expendable, parachute and propulsive (return to launch site and drone ship are one family). A contract needs at least 3 architectures with a valid build.
- **Representative per architecture (Decision).** The valid build with the lowest balanced regret `cost / budget + days / deadline + (1 − displayed success)`; ties go to higher success, lower cost, fewer days, then part IDs.
- **Dominance.** The Pareto front of the representatives on cost ↓, schedule days ↓ and displayed success ↑. *Dominant*: one architecture alone on the front. *Near-dominant*: a representative within 2% of the best representative on every axis (`cost ≤ 1.02 × min`, `days ≤ 1.02 × min`, `success ≥ 0.98 × max`). As a diagnostic the report also counts the architectures with any build on the Pareto front of **all** valid builds.
- **Archetypes (design 18.1).** Cheapest valid build (cheap and risky), highest displayed success (expensive and safe), and the highest-success build with parachute or propulsive booster recovery (best reusable).
- **Unused parts (design 18.1, Decision).** Parts that appear in no build on the Pareto front of *all* valid builds of any evaluated contract. The all-build front is used rather than the representatives' front because the representative rule favours cheap builds and would list every premium part. Parts of the non-coupled (fill) votes are only tried as the cheapest or most reliable option, so a middle option there is unused by construction; it is labelled as such.
- **Model boundaries.** Δv, TWR, mass, entry, power and data come from Core's physical or simplified-physical models. Cost (`$M`), schedule (`days`), reliability, the success estimate, and every threshold of this report (3 architectures, 2%, the regret weights) are game values.

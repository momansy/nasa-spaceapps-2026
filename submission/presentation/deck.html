Mission design is trade-offs.
Students never see them.

More instruments need more power. More power needs more mass. More mass needs more fuel, and more money.

In a classroom that chain is a table of numbers, so nobody feels a decision.

The challenge: a game where players design, fund and fly a space mission, and see why it worked or failed.

Challenge asks for

  • mass, power, budget
  • data, comms, time
  • no single perfect answer
  • feedback on why

  • Solar Wing Deploy and Eclipse Power Juggle: power
  • Ground Station Pass: communications
  • Science Capture: instruments

A fact inside the mechanic

"Many rockets throttle down near Max Q, the moment of peak aerodynamic stress, often about a minute after liftoff." Source: NASA, Space Shuttle Day-of-Launch Trajectory Design, 2011, and others.

32 minigames run on the phones. Each has a Relaxed mode and a One-Button mode. The big-screen flight view is in development.

Flight is 2D with one exponential atmosphere and probability rolls for failures. The Codex notes the simplifications.

Mission design is trade-offs.
Students never see them.

More instruments need more power, more power needs more mass, and more mass needs more fuel and money. In a classroom that is a table of numbers, so nobody feels a decision.

The challenge: a game where players design, fund and fly a space mission, and see why it worked or failed.

Flight and minigames

Constraints become short minigames

  • Solar Wing Deploy and Eclipse Power Juggle: power
  • Ground Station Pass: communications
  • Science Capture: instruments

A fact inside the mechanic

"Many rockets throttle down near Max Q, the moment of peak aerodynamic stress, often about a minute after liftoff." Source: NASA, Space Shuttle Day-of-Launch Trajectory Design, 2011, and others.

32 minigames run on the phones. Each has a Relaxed mode and a One-Button mode. The big-screen flight screen is in the main code, not yet in the hosted build.

Accessibility, languages and how it was built

Built for everyone, built by humans directing AI

6 languages, accessible by design

  • Arabic right-to-left, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Hindi, English
  • Text up to 200 percent, high contrast, reduced motion
  • Screen-reader labels; Relaxed and One-Button minigames
  • No chat, no accounts, no trackers
EnglishالعربيةEspañolFrançaisPortuguêsहिन्दी

Translations by AI agents. No native-speaker review yet.

Four parts, one protocol, tested

  • Phones: TypeScript, React, Vite
  • Room server: Node, rooms in memory
  • Big screen: Unity 6; title and lobby hosted, 15 of 16 screens in code
  • Core rules: C#, deterministic from a seed
